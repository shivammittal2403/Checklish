Client/Server

Row 8
Sr. No

Test Cases
Workstations connecting to PLCs must use the latest operating system with all patches The ICS/SCADA infrastructures and systems are protected according to principles of ‘defence in depth’: - Subject systems as minimal as possible to other network infrastructures. - Apply hardening: switch off superfluous functions and unused services, delete non-used or unnecessary user accounts and change default passwords. - See to it that adding and changing systems and configurations is a documented and controlled process. - If possible, use antivirus software and whitelisting of applications. - Create a distinction between administrator functions and user functions and the accompanying network traffic.
Impacts/Remarks
Outdated operating systems can be prone to many known exploits and obfuscated malware. As is the case with standard ICT applications, many ICS/SCADA applications are also sensitive to manipulation of the input. The chapter ‘Uitvoeringsdomein Webapplicaties’ of the ICT-Beveiligingsrichtlijnen voor webapplicaties – Verdieping (not yet available in English) offers an extensive description of these problems

012

Identify all entry points to the SCADA network which may include: • Entry points in LAN including corporate network • Internet • Wireless networking devices • Connections to business partners and vendors Limit the connections of ICS/SCADA systems with the Internet and other networks.

Row 14
Sr. No
12
Impacts/Remarks
Identifying all entry points will enable us to get the visibility of the entire SCADA network and potential attack surface. Each connection is a potential risk. Periodically (at least once a year) draw up an overview of all connections of your systems with the Internet and other networks. Carry out a risk analysis for these connections to determine the correct measures. Use security equipment such as firewalls, proxy servers and data diodes and an accompanying policy. There may be a valid reason for a connection, for example for a quick failure analysis, management or process monitoring. Information between various networks should be exchanged through a separate network segment (DMZ). Make sure to use a centrally protected facility for remote access and use two-factor authentication.
013

Multiple SCADA networks should also be segregated from one another and the corporate network. The ICS/SCADA systems make use of a separate network infrastructure. This network infrastructure is separated from other networks. The separation can be organised physically or logically.

Row 15
Sr. No
13
Impacts/Remarks
The corporate network is always connected to the Internet. Hence it increases the risk of lateral movement to the SCADA network if one of the system, server within the corporate network is compromised. If one of the SCADA networks is compromised with physical access or social engineering attack, and all SCADA networks are connected to one another, it increases a great risk of entire SCADA environment compromise. By making use of a separate network infrastructure, it is prevented that disruptions or failures and security incidents in other networks (for example, the standard office network) have a direct effect on the ICS/SCADA systems. When networks are not separated from each other, it is possible that a vulnerability in the office network may also be abused to gain access to the ICS/SCADA systems.

Take measures in order to guarantee the integrity of your configurations. - Document configurations, settings and connections and register changes that have been made. - Periodically check the actual settings/configurations with the documentation and examine possible differences. - Organise change management. Check the integrity of the software when implementing in operational systems, for example by testing changes in advance in a test environment with simulation options (FAT). - Be sure that you know that the configuration which is tested in the FAT (and approved), is also implemented accordingly in the production environment (SAT).

Many organisations have a security policy, but ICS/SCADA systems do not always fall within the scope of this policy. BYOD Policy

Row 31
Sr. No
29
Test Cases
A company may not comply in the absence of these policies. There is a policy for the use of (removable) media (such as USB sticks, hard disks and CD-ROMs) and technical measures have been taken to enforce this policy. There is a policy for connecting mobile equipment, such as laptops, tablets, smartphones, on the business networks and technical measures have been taken to enforce this policy.
Impacts/Remarks
Many virus and malware infections on ICS/SCADA systems are caused by using contaminated storage media. Explicitly include this policy in awareness campaigns. Infected equipment that is connected to the ICS/SCADA network infrastructure may cause virus and malware infections. Many suppliers use laptops for service work. This is why a total ban is not always possible in practice. Ensure that at least the system to be connected is always scanned before it is connected. You may also consider to let the external supplier only use laptops which remain under control of your own organisation (and keep these laptops on location).

Patch management policy

Row 37
Sr. No
35
Test Cases
Organise patch management: define a patch policy and remain informed about vulnerabilities, security patches and workarounds of all your system components.
Impacts/Remarks
Vulnerabilities in software are an important cause of many security problems. Suppliers of ICS/SCADA systems increasingly make use of standard software, systems and protocols. This also causes the accompanying security problems. Standard tooling and exploit kits can then be applied more easily. It is therefore necessary that all used software is up to date. Maintenance of systems, including security patches, may be part of maintenance contracts. Introducing security patches may also be part of the release management of a company and should also always be done in consultation with the system supplier.

Indicent Reporting Policy

Row 40
Sr. No
38
Test Cases
A company should have 24 hours a day incident monitoring, identification and response system, and process. Use technical tools to detect attacks. This includes a, preferably central, logging facility. This will make it easier to find out what happened in the event of incidents. Also carry out proactive inspections on this logging. In addition, make use of an Intrusion Detection System (IDS) for detecting attacks.
Impacts/Remarks
In the absence of such systems, malicious activities may go unnoticed. When reporting a hack attempt to the police, having good log files is a good thing also. An IDS can be used very well for detecting attacks because of the predictable system behaviour of many process environments.

Unauthorized entry can lead to system, server, PLC compromise. Many ICS/SCADA systems cover a larger area. In this case the weakest link determines the strength of the security chain as well.
